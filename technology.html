The architecture

Six engineering workstreams

01 / Propulsion

Lift and cruise

Compare distributed electric propulsion and alternative configurations for thrust, efficiency, controllability, noise and ground clearance.

02 / Energy

Mass and power

Build transparent mass and energy budgets. Analyze power demand for takeoff, reserve requirements, battery cooling and degradation.

03 / Aerodynamics

Two operating modes

Evaluate lift, drag, packaging and transition mechanisms without assuming that an automotive silhouette can safely fly.

04 / Flight controls

Stability first

Develop simulation-based handling requirements, fault detection and human-in-the-loop control concepts before hardware testing.

05 / Structures

Load paths

Investigate road crashworthiness alongside aircraft loads, vibration, fatigue, landing loads and structural redundancy.

06 / Operations

Human systems

Consider pilot qualification, weather limitations, vertiports, airspace access, maintenance and practical ground operation.

Safety case

Safety is a design input.

Any future demonstrator requires structured hazard identification, reliability assessment, verification plans, emergency procedures and independent review.

Evidence we would need

Configuration-specific hazard analysis; redundancy and failure-mode studies; test plans and reports; flight-test authorization; operational limitations; traceability from requirements to validated evidence.

No prototype flight, certification, or safety performance has been demonstrated by the information published here.

Regulatory pathway

Road and aviation compliance

In Europe, an intended aircraft configuration would require early discussion with the relevant aviation authorities, including EASA where applicable; road approval requires separate analysis under applicable vehicle rules. The applicable certification basis depends on vehicle design, operating concept and jurisdiction.

Step 1: define category

Agree intended operations, vehicle category and certification strategy with qualified aviation/regulatory specialists.

Step 2: map standards

Identify relevant airworthiness, systems assurance, noise, environmental and road-safety requirements.

Step 3: prove compliance

Evidence from engineering reviews, simulations, lab work, ground tests and authorized flight testing—not renderings.
