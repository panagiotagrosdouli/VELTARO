# VELTARO business plan — founder review draft

## 1. Executive Summary

VELTARO is an independent concept-stage venture. First product hypothesis: VELTARO LABS Preliminary Mobility Feasibility Research Brief; V1 remains long-term dual-architecture R&D. This plan is a founder review draft dated 13 November 2026. Incorporation, revenue, funding, staff and customer traction are unverified. Near-term objective: create reproducible evidence and validate a narrow customer problem before capital commitments.

## 2. Vision and Mission

Develop credible advanced mobility research with traceable assumptions, responsible safety boundaries and staged capital allocation.

## 3. Problem Definition

Mobility innovation teams, early engineering teams and research project managers may face costly uncertainty in early technology decisions, fragmented technical literature and architecture assumptions. Confirm whether this problem warrants an external desk brief.

## 4. Proposed Solution

VELTARO LABS Preliminary Mobility Feasibility Research Brief; V1 remains long-term dual-architecture R&D. Software prototypes demonstrate workflow only. No physical-product, regulated engineering or operational permission claim.

## 5. Product Portfolio

LABS first prospective commercial workstream. V1 flagship research; AIR aviation, SKYCOURIER aerial logistics and GROUND delivery/mobility remain concepts. Separate each mission and risk boundary.

## 6. Market Research

Use the dated source register below. Official EASA and EU frameworks show distinct road and aircraft approval questions. Public competitor pages establish research categories, not validated V1 demand. TAM/SAM/SOM: TBD. Obtain segment counts, procurement budgets, interview records and bottom-up reachable account estimates before quoting market size.

## 7. Customer Segments

Mobility innovation teams, early engineering teams and research project managers. Buyer, budget owner, operator and affected user may differ. Accessibility and privacy stakeholders must participate. No customer segment is validated.

## 8. Competitor Analysis

Joby is an aviation landscape example; PAL-V illustrates a different road/air concept. Neither validates V1 economics or certification. LABS alternatives include internal engineering staff, university research/transfer groups such as DLR and established specialist consultancies. This is a preliminary landscape, not an exhaustive competitive or pricing study. Compare competence, evidence review, scope and turnaround using actual quotations before positioning against them.

## 9. Business Model

Test a fixed-scope LABS desk brief first. Intake → competency/conflict screening → scope/contract → public-source research → evidence and uncertainty register → qualified review → export → customer acceptance. Price by bounded effort only after interviews and legal trading review. V1/AIR/SKYCOURIER/GROUND consume research capital; no vehicle revenue is included in the planning base case.

## 10. Go-to-Market Strategy

First 30 days: create consent-based interview list; define five problem questions and evidence criteria. Days 31–60: seek founder-authorized interviews and record current workaround, procurement requirements, objections and willingness-to-pay. Days 61–90: decide whether to stop, narrow scope or pursue a legal, supervised pilot. Interview targets are targets, never reported as completed. No outreach was sent by this work.

## 11. Technical Development Roadmap

LABS gate: reproducible sample, evidence review and scope competence. V1 gate: common lawful mission and comparable uncertainty inputs for VTOL and folding fixed wing. Both remain HOLD. Close pack-level mass, usable energy, peak power/thermal constraints, conversion hazards and infrastructure permissions before any architecture selection. Software arithmetic cannot authorize flights or establish airworthiness.

## 12. Operations

Founder is decision owner, not proof of a staffed company. Assign software reviewer, engineering safety reviewer, privacy reviewer and finance/accounting reviewer before release. Maintain source/claim register and reviewed pull requests. No public client information in repository. Local tools are internal prototypes; external hosting/auth/storage require a separate privacy and security design.

## 13. Funding Requirements

Use financial-model.xlsx to estimate a gate-bounded cash requirement. No actual investment ask or valuation is justified yet. Obtain written engineering, hardware, legal and insurance quotes; confirm hiring capacity and taxes. EIC Accelerator is a public research lead only; eligibility, live call deadlines, legal status and co-financing remain unverified.

## 14. Financial Model

Editable workbook contains 36-month low/base/high assumptions, monthly costs, hypothetical receipts, gross margin, cash, runway flag, break-even and funding gap. Every entered number is an unquoted planning assumption, not a verified expense or forecast. Base receipts are zero. Hardware feasibility and specialist review are scoped allowances, not a complete vehicle/robot development budget. VAT, financing, depreciation and corporate tax are excluded pending accountant input.

## 15. Risks

Principal risks: no demonstrated buyer need, insufficient qualified delivery capacity, inaccurate technical assumptions, privacy and image rights, unclear IP ownership, legal trading ability and capital shortfall. Road/air conversion, loss of lift, energy thermal limits, certification uncertainty and infrastructure access require qualified review.

## 16. Milestones

M1 (0–30 days target): reproduce repository tests and review scope. M2 (31–60): complete missing engineering integrations or bounded LABS delivery rehearsal. M3 (61–90): independently review evidence, costs and discovery findings. M4: founder decides continuation using measured gate evidence. These windows are planning targets, not delivery promises. See READINESS.md for actual status.
