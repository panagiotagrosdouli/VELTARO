# VELTARO — Commercial validation playbook v1.0
**Prepared:** 2026-10-19 | **Stage:** Concept research, pre-incorporation | **Cash available:** €0 | **Jurisdiction:** Undecided

## Executive decision
VELTARO is one proposed brand with four research tracks, **not four incorporated companies**. The near-term objective is to learn which problem is worth solving before spending on hardware, registration, trademarks, domains, advertising, subscriptions, or speculative technical claims. The first possible commercial wedge is a *narrow non-regulated VELTARO LABS research deliverable*, contingent on customer demand and lawful invoicing. This is a testable hypothesis, not an established business.

## Target customers and problems
| Track | Interview target | Problem to validate | First evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | Road/aviation engineers, potential premium users, relevant advisors | Is dual road-and-air mobility desirable and technically/regulatorily realistic? | 3 problem interviews and a documented mass/energy constraint brief |
| AIR | Aircraft operators, training or mobility stakeholders | Is a compact passenger mission valuable under real airspace rules? | 3 mission interviews and regulatory questions |
| SKYCOURIER | Logistics operators and delivery businesses | Which constrained delivery mission, if any, improves cost/service? | 3 operator interviews; model including operating restrictions |
| LABS | Early-stage engineering teams, research groups, small manufacturers | Would they value scoped desk research, competitor mapping or feasibility synthesis? | 3 buyer interviews and a sample non-confidential report |

**Interview target:** 12 total by day 90, distributed as above (3 per track), not 12 per track. The earlier founding strategy's 12-interviews-in-30-days scenario is aspirational and superseded here by the €0 plan's staged cadence.

## Discovery script (do not pitch first)
1. What was the last time this problem occurred? What did it cost in effort, delay, or money?
2. How did you solve it? Which alternatives did you actually use?
3. Who owns the budget and who approves a solution?
4. What evidence would you need before considering a new supplier or concept?
5. May we ask one follow-up question? Record consent without publishing personally identifying data.

Document date, track, audience segment, factual observation, evidence strength, uncertainty, and next action. Don't mislabel statements of interest as purchase intent; do not claim traction from interviews alone.

## Value proposition experiment — LABS
Proposed test offer: a **non-regulated, desk-based technical landscape / early feasibility research brief** with defined scope, sources, assumptions and limitations. Deliver one free demonstrator using public information. Ask three prospects whether such a report addresses a real workflow and what would make it purchase-worthy. **No paid engagement, invoices, deposits or representations of engineering certification** until legal/tax and competency requirements are checked in the actual jurisdiction.

## Evidence and portfolio scoring
After 90 days, score each track 0–5 in customer evidence (25%), technical feasibility (25%), regulatory feasibility (20%), capital efficiency (20%), talent/partner access (10%). A missing evidence category scores **0**, not an optimistic estimate. Record a source and uncertainty for every nonzero score; weights are an internal hypothesis, not industry standard. Choose one primary workstream only if its evidence clears the gates; otherwise continue research or stop.

## External communications guardrails
- Say “concept-stage research venture” and label renderings as concept visuals.
- No prototype, certified aircraft, guaranteed flight performance, investor backing, signed partners, customer numbers or incorporation claims without proof.
- No confidential outreach through public GitHub Issues.
- Contact form uses a third-party processor; confirm owner activation, consent wording, retention policy and applicable privacy law before promoting it.
- Seek qualified regulatory, legal, tax and engineering review before regulated operations or financial commitments.
